What owning looks like

Own what you see

A .eth name is proven by a light client on your machine, and every IPFS block is checked against its hash. Servers can withhold. They can't lie.

Own what apps can do

An app lists what it needs, and you read it in plain words and decide. Whatever it didn't declare, it can never get, not even from you.

Own who you trust

A shield in the address bar tells you what you're on: plain Web2, content you can verify, or open and trustless Web3. You choose who does the scoring.

Own your data

No account, no cloud, no sync service. Your keys and data stay on your machine.

Your everyday browser, too

Built to be lived in, not opened only when you need a dapp.

  • Full uBlock Origin

    The complete MV2 version, the one Chrome no longer runs.

  • Chrome extensions

    From the Chrome Web Store, a .crx or a folder.

  • Profiles and private windows

    Each one a separate browser.

  • Tabs that scale

    Groups, split view, sleeping tabs, session restore.

  • Private by default

    Global Privacy Control, HTTPS-only, DNS over HTTPS, third-party cookies blocked.

  • Bring your bookmarks

    Import from Chrome, Edge or Firefox. Passwords stay in your OS keyring.

Linux first. Native on X11 and Wayland, with Windows and macOS too.

Your wallet extension works here as it does in Chrome. A native wallet, with its own security model, is on the way.

What the web forbids, granted by you

  • A capability broker checks everythingEvery socket, file and signature is checked against your grant, on every call.
  • Real sockets from a pageTCP, TLS and UDP: connect, connectSecure, listen, udpBind.
  • Your Node.js app, unchangedThe Lounge's own Express, Socket.IO and SQLite server runs inside the tab.
  • Native code only as WebAssemblyAddons load as WebAssembly and programs run as WASI.

macOS

Detected

macOS 13 Ventura or newer · Apple silicon or Intel

Recommended

One command in Terminal

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OrivonBrowser/orivon-mvp/main/scripts/install-macos.sh | bash
  1. 1Open Terminal: press ⌘ Space, type Terminal, press Return.
  2. 2Paste the command with ⌘ V and press Return.
  3. 3Orivon installs into Applications and opens. No password needed.

The script picks the right build for Apple silicon or Intel, checks the download’s SHA-256 against the digest GitHub publishes, never uses sudo, and opens without a Gatekeeper prompt. Read the script.
